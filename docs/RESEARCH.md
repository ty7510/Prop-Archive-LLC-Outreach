**The trigger (asked as a question about their records, never as "the law requires")**

From then a text is a "telephone solicitation" unless the recipient has an established business relationship within 12 months (a voluntary two-way communication on the basis of an inquiry, plus consent to continue beyond the inquiry) or gave prior express written consent; hours 9 AM to 7 PM, no Sunday, no legal holiday; STOP, QUIT, END, REVOKE, OPT OUT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE must be honored. Before Oct 19 an oral express request still excludes a call. ## Each approach in two paragraphs

### How comparable small-landlord software got its first 10 customers

This approach looks at how TurboTenant, Avail, RentRedi, Hemlane, Baselane, Stessa, DoorLoop, Innago, TenantCloud, Azibo, Landlord Studio, Buildium, Rentec Direct and Cozy got their first paying landlords, and turns the pattern into a play for Theo: get introduced by someone a Philadelphia landlord already trusts, meet in person at the landlord's table at a trigger moment (a new building, a bad repair or dispute, tax season, the Safe Healthy Homes law that bites on Nov 1, 2026), set Prop Archive up on the spot with their real paperwork in the style of Paul Graham's "Collison installation" (https://paulgraham.com/ds.html), ask for the subscription before leaving, and ask every yes for two named introductions. Since Theo has no landlord network, he borrows one at DIG, HAPCO, Jumpstart and city landlord events, plus the landlords he and his Drexel friends already pay rent to in 19104. The evidence for the direction is real: of 23 fast-growing B2B companies, 12 got their first ten customers through the founders' own network and 9 by going where customers gathered, and "getting press is rarely the way to get started" (https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-businesses); Buildium's founders got early customers "by pounding the pavement and talking to the property managers that we knew" (https://www.buildium.com/news/buildiums-michael-monteiro-on-bootstrapping-to-6k-customers); Innago's 170-unit Cincinnati customer heard of it from another landlord and was onboarded personally by the founder (https://innago.com/case-study/ben-properties/); Avail signed "maybe five landlords a month" early on before SEO took over (https://mixergy.com/interviews/avail-with-ryan-coon/); Rentec Direct grew from a free product that landlords "shared with their friends" (https://mixergy.com/interviews/rentec-direct-with-nathan-miller/); TenantCloud started in 2014 and saw its first revenue in 2018 (https://starterstory.com/stories/we-launched-a-12m-year-property-management-saas-for-landlords-tenants); and referred customers are worth at least 16% more and retain better (https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.75.1.46). The Philadelphia pool is sized from live L&I data: 94,092 active rental licenses, 1,646 Ray-band owners (16-60 units) citywide and 536 in the seven target zips, and 142 owners with 5-60 units who licensed a new property in the last 90 days (https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/licenses-and-inspections-business-licenses/, https://phl.carto.com/api/v2/sql). The researcher put this at 3 sit-downs and 15 hours per paying customer with a first customer in 4 weeks.

Both skeptics refuted those numbers and the synthesis settled on 6 introduced sit-downs and about 48 founder-hours per paying customer, with 8 to 12 weeks to the first one (estimate, since no comparable reports a sit-down-to-paid ratio). The evidence skeptic found that none of the fourteen comparables documents a paid close in one sitting: every referral story is about a free or near-free product with a slow, staged migration, the Lenny sample is founders' own colleagues and investors with survivorship bias, the 50-70% referral win rate is an unlinked vendor compilation (https://growsurf.com/statistics/b2b-referral-marketing-statistics/), and the Schmitt et al. paper is about retention value, not close rate. The practical skeptic anchored the close rate on Founding Sales, where "win rates in the 10% or 20% range will be more typical" and a one-call close is "pretty unlikely at the beginning" (https://www.foundingsales.com/8-closing, https://www.foundingsales.com/7-pitching), and noted that Innago's referred landlord let years pass and then piloted a handful of units first (https://innago.com/case-study/ben-properties/), while student-housing operators, Theo's 19104 lever, were the buyers Innago's founder found had "little reason to buy" (https://www.wcpo.com/news/insider/innagos-innovative-software-could-be-a-boon-for-landlords-with-a-small-to-medium-number-of-units). The approach recruits the professionals who meet a Philadelphia landlord at a mandatory, paper-heavy moment (EPA-certified lead dust-sampling technicians, landlord-side eviction attorneys, landlord CPAs in the January to April tax window, and DSCR lenders or investor agents as the "just bought another property" feeders), asks each for one or two named intros rather than a referral program, and closes the introduced landlord by setting up their first property live in the intro meeting, Paul Graham's "Right then, give me your laptop" move (http://paulgraham.com/ds.html). The wedge is real and re-verified by both skeptics: the city makes every landlord produce the same stack on a clock, a $63-per-unit rental license valid 365 days and renewable 45 days early, with no rent collectable without it (https://www.phila.gov/media/20231204173843/Rental-License-Requirements_Nov-2023.pdf), a lead-safe certificate from an EPA dust-sampling technician that goes to the tenant, is uploaded to the city and expires every four years (https://www.phila.gov/media/20210223163004/FAQ-Final.pdf), and the L&I license table shows 94,092 active rental licenses, 4,337 owner names with 3 to 6 licenses, 923 with 7 to 14, and 34,185 licenses (36%) expiring in February 2027 (https://phl.carto.com/api/v2/sql?q=SELECT+to_char(expirationdate,%27YYYY-MM%27)+AS+exp_month,+count(*)+AS+n+FROM+business_licenses+WHERE+licensetype=%27Rental%27+AND+licensestatus=%27Active%27+GROUP+BY+exp_month+ORDER+BY+n+DESC+LIMIT+12&format=json). The partners sit where landlords already gather: DIG's main meeting (645 members, every third Thursday, first visit free, none in November or December, https://digonline.org/) lists a lead-compliance firm, two tax firms, title, insurance and a Philadelphia DSCR lender among 38 vendors and no software vendor (https://digonline.org/VendorListings.aspx), and HAPCO represents around 1,000 city landlords (https://whyy.org/articles/philadelphia-court-settlement-renter-protections/). The bounty was anchored on Hemlane's $250 per customer (https://www.hemlane.com/referral-program/), RentRedi's up to 20% of a first purchase (https://rentredi.com/affiliate-program/) and TurboTenant's $40 (https://www.turbotenant.com/affiliates/); the only peer-reviewed referral evidence shows referred customers are about 18% more likely to stay and worth 16 to 25% more, but measures value and retention, not conversion, and its authors warn paid B2B referrals can look like a bribe (https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=9643). The researcher's headline of 6 conversations, 20 hours and 5 weeks per customer rested on two estimates: a 40% close rate built from HockeyStack's 20.7% blended B2B SaaS close rate (https://hockeystack.com/lab-blog-posts/close-rates-deal-sizes-and-churn-rates) times Crossbeam's unsourced 53% partner uplift (https://insider.crossbeam.com/resources/4-ways-partner-sourced-leads-outperform-cold-leads), and a 1-in-3 partner activation rate with no source at all.

Both skeptics refuted the numbers and kept the wedge. The evidence skeptic found that 20.7% x 1.53 is 31.7%, not 40%, that the baseline comes from $9M to $120M ARR sales teams and not a zero-customer founder, that the page cited for partner activation holds no partner statistic and shows 0 of 23 first-10-customer stories partner-sourced (https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-businesses), and that "paying account" cannot happen because the live site says "Free during the beta. No card." with no dollar figure (https://proparchive.app/pricing); he corrected the cost to about 12 conversations and 40 hours per signed (not paying) customer and 8 weeks to the first, with a roughly one-in-three chance (estimate) that the single Oct 15 DIG evening yields no active partner before DIG goes dark until Jan 21, 2027. He also found HAPCO's live site (https://hapcophiladelphia.com) lists dues of $190 a year, not $125, an annual rather than monthly general meeting, "Ask the Attorney" as a members-only Zoom call, and one in-person room in the window, Bridge the Gap on Oct 20 at Esperanza Arts Center. The practical skeptic corrected to about 16 conversations and 55 hours per customer across the first three (roughly 24 and 95 for the very first) and 10 to 14 weeks to the first paying customer, with hidden costs the plan did not count: every partner is two conversations, not one; 20 to 30 solo-developer hours (estimate) of reminder, partner-listing and attribution features before the pitch is honest; a no-card live account benchmarks at the 18.2% opt-in trial-to-paid rate, not 40%, against 48.8% with a card on file (https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-benchmarks/); Pennsylvania attorneys may not pay or be paid for referrals under Rule 7.2(b) (https://www.padisciplinaryboard.org/for-attorneys/rules/rule/3/the-rules-of-professional-conduct) and CPAs need the fee disclosed in a client-signed engagement letter under 49 Pa. ### Cold email, cold call and LinkedIn as the baseline to beat

This is the classic outbound motion, built on public records instead of a bought list: pull every active Rental license from the L&I Business Licenses table (https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/licenses-and-inspections-business-licenses/) and owner mailing addresses from the OPA properties table (https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/philadelphia-properties-and-assessment-history/), keep owners of 3 to 60 units in the seven target zips, tag two triggers (a new license or a multi-family sale since July 2026), resolve each LLC to a person, then run a three-touch 60 to 90 word email sequence at no more than about 30 sends per inbox per day (https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/), a Wednesday or Thursday afternoon call block, and LinkedIn only for Denise-type management-company owners. The data side is real and reproducible: on 2026-10-11 the Carto API showed 94,092 active rental licenses, 1,576 owner names holding 16 to 60 units citywide and 474 owners of 3 to 60 units who added a license since 2026-07-01 (https://phl.carto.com/api/v2/sql?q=SELECT%20count(*)%20AS%20growing_owners_3_60%2C%20sum(u)%20AS%20units%20FROM%20(SELECT%20opa_owner%2C%20sum(numberofunits)%20AS%20u%2C%20count(*)%20AS%20licenses%2C%20max(initialissuedate)%20AS%20latest%20FROM%20business_licenses%20WHERE%20licensetype%3D%27Rental%27%20AND%20licensestatus%3D%27Active%27%20AND%20opa_owner%20IS%20NOT%20NULL%20GROUP%20BY%20opa_owner)%20t%20WHERE%20u%20BETWEEN%203%20AND%2060%20AND%20licenses%20%3E%3D%202%20AND%20latest%20%3E%3D%20%272026-07-01%27). The conversion evidence is what sinks it. Belkins' 2025 study of 7,530,489 strict cold emails found a 0.45% reply rate and 1,200+ appointments, which works out to roughly 6,300 emails per booked meeting; owners and 0 to 10 employee firms replied best at 0.57% and 0.72% (https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates). Belkins' 175,000-dial calling funnel shows 9.9% connect, 4.6% conversation-to-meeting and about 370 dials per meeting (https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks), Cognism's success rate fell to 2.3% in 2025 (https://www.cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-statistics), and 86% of calls from unknown numbers go unanswered (https://www.hiya.com/state-of-the-call). LinkedIn runs at 29.6% connection acceptance and about 10% reply among Expandi's mostly software users (https://expandi.io/?p=14040), but only about 32% of US adults use it and usage skews to higher income and college degrees, so it misses most rowhouse owners (https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/, https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/who-uses-facebook-instagram-linkedin-and-twitter/). The researcher reached 20 conversations, 45 hours and 7 weeks per customer only by swapping Belkins for unattributed vendor tables that claim 1 to 2.5 meetings per 100 emails (https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cold-email-benchmarks-by-industry) and a trigger-hook lift with no disclosed sample (https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/), and by assuming a 15 to 25% demo close drawn from a vendor compilation of referenceable sellers (https://optif.ai/learn/questions/demo-to-close-conversion-rate/). Lenny Rachitsky's study of first-ten-customer stories found every company used personal network or going where customers already are, with cold email only as a sub-tactic by founders who already had reputations (https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-businesses).

Both skeptics refuted the numbers. The evidence skeptic re-ran every Carto query (they reproduce) and corrected the approach as written, with the calling leg, to about 140 conversations and 180 hours per paying customer, with a 12 to 20 week median to the first one and a 37 to 55% chance that a 500-contact cohort yields nobody (Poisson, the skeptic's own calculation, an estimate); the practical skeptic put the email-only path at about 60 conversations (range 30 to 85) and 200 hours per customer, and the head of go-to-market settled on 100 conversations and 190 hours. The hidden costs they surfaced are concrete: 266 of the 474 trigger owners are companies and the L&I table carries no phone or email, Pennsylvania LLC filings need not name any member (https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-15-pacsa-corporations-and-unincorporated-associations/pa-csa-sect-15-8821/), bulk skip tracing starts at $2,000 a month (https://batchdata.io/pricing) and cheaper per-record tools still cost $58 a month or 15 to 17 cents a hit (https://www.skipgenie.com/, https://www.reiskip.com/), so manual resolution runs 50 to 80 hours per 500 contacts (estimate, no source measures this). A new domain needs at least 14 days of warm-up with no campaigns (https://www.mailreach.co/blog/email-warm-up), the 30-per-inbox cap stretches one 1,500-send cohort to 50 inbox-days, and each inbox is $7 a month plus a $47 a month sending tool (https://workspace.google.com/pricing, https://instantly.ai/pricing). The hyper-local pool is tiny: only 180 of the 474 trigger owners hold a license in the seven zips, about 60 of them person-named and roughly 3 of those Ray-sized, so the local list is spent after one or two customers. Every cold demo also has to beat free competitors (https://www.stessa.com/pricing/, https://www.baselane.com/pricing/, https://innago.com/pricing/, https://www.turbotenant.com/pricing/, https://rentredi.com/pricing/), and the researcher's "business lines are DNC-exempt" premise fails in Pennsylvania: the Attorney General requires telemarketer registration ($500 for two years plus a $50,000 bond; the B2B exemption needs three years under the same name) and a $495 a year Do Not Call list purchase whether or not exempt (https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/?p=9150, https://hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2019/10/pennsylvania-gives-telephone-subscribers-new-protections), and SB 992, effective October 18 or 19, 2026, pulls texts and ringless voicemail into "telephone solicitation" with penalties up to $1,000 per violation (https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/09/pennsylvania-strengthens-telemarketing-protections/, https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/state-telemarketing-update-what-to-know-about-changes-coming-in-pennsylvania). The final read for Theo: this is the floor every other approach must beat, roughly 30 to 70 times off his 5-of-10 target, so keep the L&I and OPA queries as a free way to verify and prepare for warm conversations, drop the calling leg entirely, and never run cold outbound as the main channel.

## What to stop doing

- Stop running the free-beta, no-card, 6-months-free offer as the only offer. Until /pricing shows a price and a way to pay, no channel can produce a paying customer; today it reads "Free during the beta. No card." with no dollar figure (https://proparchive.app/pricing, re-verified 2026-10-11; client/src/content/offer.ts: "No dollar figure, ever: there is no price yet").
- Stop counting a beta sign-up, a booked demo or a compliment as a customer. Count a charged card (or, as the stated fallback, a card on file) plus use at day 30. "Most of your meetings will end with a compliment ... they're almost certainly lying" (The Mom Test p.28, https://theforge.mcmaster.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/The-Mom-Test.pdf); no-card trials convert 4 to 6% at the median (https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-rate/).
- Stop cold email, cold calling and LinkedIn to strangers for 14 days, and keep the outreach system in /home/theo/code/Prop-Archive-LLC-Outreach (docs/SPEC.md) in dry run as a lookup layer for the warm 10. Cold email runs about 6,300 emails per booked appointment (https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates), cold calls about 370 dials per meeting (https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks), and the calling leg triggers Pennsylvania registration ($500 plus a $50,000 bond), a $495/yr Do Not Call list purchase even if exempt, and from Oct 19, 2026 Act 47's text and voicemail rules at up to $1,000 per violation (https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/?p=9150, https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/state-telemarketing-update-what-to-know-about-changes-coming-in-pennsylvania). LEGAL.md covers CAN-SPAM and TCPA but not the Pennsylvania Act.
- Stop mailing letters to newly licensed owners from the L&I feed as a first touch: the trigger fires after compliance (0 of the brand-new 2-15 unit licensees have a lead cert expiring within 120 days, https://services.arcgis.com/fLeGjb7u4uXqeF9q/arcgis/rest/services/lhhp_lead_certifications/FeatureServer/0/query), the Maya-size pool is about 2 owners a week, and owner-addressed investor mail runs 0.4% per touch (https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/898/topics/236330-040-response-rate-on-yellow-letter---looking-for-suggestions?page=1). Zelle stays on day one; records are the sale.
- Stop opening with features or with "the new law requires". Open with the owner's last bad day and one question; mention Nov 1 once, as a date. HAPCO opposed the bills (https://whyy.org/articles/philadelphia-tenant-protection-retaliatory-evictions-inspections-legislation) and a federal suit is pending (https://www.flastergreenberg.com/newsroom-alerts-Philadelphia-Landlords-Prepare-Now-for-New-Compliance-Requirements-and-Increased-Liability-Under-Recently-Passed-City-Legislation.html); Theo is not a lawyer.
